For my final, I propose Murmuration, a single-user virtual reality experience. I’ve always loved birds. After growing up with screeching birdy siblings, I sometimes wonder how. My family’s parrots, Tommy and Gandy, are up at the crack of dawn every day, yelling and banging for their breakfast veggies. They get jealous of each other and will hold a grudge. Last winter, I watched a movie with Gandy on my lap. Later, when I was putting them to bed, Tommy punished me, biting my finger hard. Over a year later, she is still getting reparations, ever since that night she poops on my shoulder when I’m home. They have personalities and moods, just like people. Unlike squirrels, deer, and other animals I see regularly, birds feel like individuals, each with their own routine, home, and family. It’s why flocks amaze me. I’m used to talking to Gandy or seeing a sparrow or two hopping around outside, but when an incomprehensible number of crows fly overhead, I have to stop and watch. My goal for Murmuration was to capture the awe inspired by the largest of flocks on Earth.
